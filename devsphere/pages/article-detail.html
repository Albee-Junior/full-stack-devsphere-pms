Introducing the DevSphere AI Project Planner

Turn any project brief into an editable task breakdown, milestone plan, and proposed timeline — grounded in the context of the project you're already working on.

MC
Maya Chen Nov 4, 2024 · 6 min read

Planning a project has always required two distinct skills: deciding what needs to happen and turning that decision into something a team can execute. The first is a product judgment call. The second is mostly busywork.

Today we're introducing the DevSphere AI Project Planner — a workspace-aware assistant that takes a project brief and returns a structured, editable plan: task breakdown, milestones, dependencies, and a proposed timeline. The output isn't a suggestion in a chat window. It's real, editable project structure that appears on your board, in your timeline, and in your reports.

How it works

The Project Planner operates on the project you select. When you open it from a project's AI workspace, it already knows:

  • Existing tasks, milestones, and their statuses
  • The team's current workload and assignment history
  • Project goals, deadlines, and stated scope
  • Files and documents attached to the project

That context is what makes the output materially different from a generic AI chat. The assistant can reason about what's already in progress, what's blocked, and where new work would realistically fit.

Breaking down a brief

Paste a project description, an RFP, or a few paragraphs of context. The assistant returns a proposed task list — each item with a suggested priority, an estimated duration, dependencies where they apply, and a suggested owner drawn from people already on the project.

You review before anything is created. Suggested tasks appear in an editable preview, not on your board. Accept individual items, edit them, or reject the whole thing. Nothing persists to your workspace until you confirm.

Milestones and timeline

The planner also proposes milestones. For a project like the Campus Learning Portal, the milestone plan might look like:

  • Alpha Release — feature-complete internal build
  • Beta Release — external testing with pilot users
  • Public Launch — general availability

Each milestone is accompanied by a proposed date based on the task dependencies and the team's current workload. The proposal is a starting point — every date is editable before the plan is accepted.

Risk and dependency summary

Before finalizing, the planner produces a short risk summary: where the plan is likely to slip, which tasks are on the critical path, and which owners appear over-committed. This is where the workspace context matters most — the assistant can flag conflicts against your existing workload, not just against the plan itself.

"The plan is a starting point. The value is that the assistant already understands your workspace, so the first draft is usually close to right."

What changes for teams

Most teams spend hours translating a brief into an initial plan. That time is often invisible — no one lists "create 40 tasks with estimates and dependencies" in the project kickoff. But it's real, and it's the single biggest source of friction between a project idea and a project that's actually moving.

The Project Planner does not remove the need for planning judgment. It removes the mechanical work of structuring the plan so your team can focus on the decisions that actually matter.

Availability

The Project Planner is available today for every workspace on Pro plans and above. It's also available for Free plan workspaces with a monthly usage cap. You can access it from any project through the AI Project Workspace entry point.

We're actively iterating — if you have feedback on the output or suggestions for how the planner could be more useful in your workflow, please share it through the in-product feedback control or on the DevSphere community.
